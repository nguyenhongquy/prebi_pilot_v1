# Feedback Quality Rating Protocol 0.1.0 (Development Draft)

Status: development draft. Calibrate on development examples before freezing or using for confirmatory ratings.

## Purpose

Rate the quality of one displayed feedback output for one reflection. Judge the feedback itself against the supplied reflection and rubric. Do not infer the condition, model, or author. Do not compare with another condition while assigning these ratings.

## Materials shown

The packet provides the segmented reflection, segment IDs, rubric and scoring anchors, and one feedback output. It does not show gold segment annotations, expert reference scores, human feedback, condition, model, or intermediate analysis.

## Rating scale

For each criterion choose one integer from 1 to 5, or select `unable_to_judge` and explain why. Use the full scale; do not leave a blank merely because the output is imperfect.

1. Very poor: materially wrong, unsupported, or unusable.
2. Poor: major problems; only limited useful content.
3. Mixed: partly useful, with noticeable issues.
4. Good: mostly sound and useful, with minor issues.
5. Excellent: consistently sound, specific, and useful.

## Criteria

- Correctness: Are claims about the reflection and rubric accurate? Does the feedback avoid invented events, motives, strengths, or deficits?
- Evidence grounding: Are claims supported by the reflection? Do cited segment IDs point to relevant evidence? Lack of a citation is not automatically an error if the claim is clearly grounded.
- Specificity: Does the feedback identify the relevant behavior or reasoning rather than rely on generic advice?
- Actionability: Are next steps concrete, feasible, and connected to the identified need?
- Reflection potential: Could the feedback help the learner reconsider assumptions, decisions, evidence, or alternatives without dictating a single answer?

## Span comments

Optionally highlight an exact span in the displayed generated feedback and add a free-text comment. Optional tags: `unsupported`, `inaccurate`, `vague`, `actionable`, `strength`, `concern`, or `other`. Highlighted feedback offsets refer to the exact displayed output. Link reflection segment IDs separately when identifying source evidence. Do not alter the displayed output while annotating.

## Overall comments

Use the packet-level comment for issues not tied to one span. Keep the independent criterion scores even if an adjudication occurs later. Do not expose names, inferred conditions, or outside information.

## Interpretation

These are quality judgments, not ground-truth rubric scores. Rate each criterion independently; do not let polished style compensate for factual errors.
